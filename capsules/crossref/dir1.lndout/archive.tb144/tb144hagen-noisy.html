Title: Getting noisy

Summary: implementing and refining Perlin noise in ConTeXt, MetaPost, Lua, and C.

Full text of article: available to TUG members (join TUG); will be publicly available after the next issue is published.

Authors: Hans Hagen, Keith McKay

Publication: TUGboat volume 46, number 3 (2025), pages 364–368

DOI (this page): 10.47397/tb/46-3/tb144hagen-noisy
(previous doi - next doi)

Category: Graphics

Difficulty: Advanced

Abstract: It all started as an experiment. Keith, whilst aimlessly searching the Internet looking for inspiration for a generative art project, came across some nice random graphics using Perlin noise. Delving further he found that the creator of Perlin noise, Ken Perlin, was the recipient of an Oscar in 1996 for the development of Perlin noise, a technique used to produce natural appearing textures on computer generated surfaces for motion picture visual effects. Perlin originally developed the idea of pseudo-random noise whilst working on the computer graphics for the film Tron in 1982.

This became the seed for thinking about creating interesting pseudo-random graphics using Perlin noise in Metafun, the graphical subsystem that ConTeXt provides. Here we will follow the steps that brought us native noise generation in ConTeXt. It is a story about experimenting, getting hooked, exploring performance gain and eventually interfacing.
