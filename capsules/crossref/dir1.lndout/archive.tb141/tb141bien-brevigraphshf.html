Title: Identifying glyphs in some 16th century fonts: Hochfeder's font no. 2, a case study

Summary: discussion of encoding selected Latin brevigraphs in a 16th century font by Kaspar Hochfeder.

Full text of article: publicly available now.

Author: Janusz S. Bień

Publication: TUGboat volume 45, number 3 (2024), pages 317–323

DOI (this page): 10.47397/tb/45-3/tb141bien-brevigraphshf
(previous doi - next doi)

Category: Humanities

Difficulty: Advanced

Abstract: Selected characters from 16th century fonts are discussed in the paper, notably Kaspar Hochfeder’s font no. 2. In particular their representation in the Unicode standard is investigated. For some of them the encoding is obvious, but there are also characters for which their encoding remains an open question. Some old characters don’t have Unicode codepoints, but can be printed with an appropriate OpenType/TrueType fonts using typographic features. Two such fonts are used: Junicode and Incunable. Some characters from Gutenberg’s bible are also mentioned.
