Title: Japanese TeX in 2026: An outside-in perspective

Summary: snapshot of the Japanese TeX ecosystem: typesetting, engines, futures.

Full text of article: available to TUG members (join TUG); will be publicly available after the next issue is published.

Author: Takuto Asakura

Publication: TUGboat volume 47, number 3 (2026), pages 312–320

DOI (this page): 10.47397/tb/47-3/tb147asakura-jptex
(previous doi - next doi)

Category: Multilingual Documents

Difficulty: Intermediate Plus

Abstract: The Japanese TeX ecosystem has developed largely on its own terms, and much of it remains opaque outside Japan: the engines are widely used domestically but rarely elsewhere, and English documentation is scarce.

This article offers a snapshot of that ecosystem in 2026 for an international audience. It reviews what makes Japanese typesetting structurally different from Western typesetting, describes the two workflows in everyday use—pTeX family and LuaTeX-ja—and the different philosophies behind them, and examines the challenges the community now faces: accumulated technical debt, the pace of LaTeX kernel evolution, severely limited maintainer capacity, and the still-tentative npTeX concept. It is offered from the perspective of an observer of both the local Japanese and the global TeX communities, and is based on a talk given at TUG 2026.
