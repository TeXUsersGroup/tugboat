Title: Endless forms most literate

Summary: from Pierre-Arnoul de Marneffe's holons to Graham Nelson's Inweb.

Full text of article: available to TUG members (join TUG); will be publicly available after the next issue is published.

Author: Mitchell Gerrard

Publication: TUGboat volume 47, number 2 (2026), pages 254–257

DOI (this page): 10.47397/tb/47-2/tb146gerrard-literate
(previous doi - next doi)

Category: Literate Programming

Difficulty: Intermediate Plus

Abstract: Donald Knuth’s WEB system of literate programming spawned many other WEB-like frameworks that worked, well, mostly like WEB. But one of WEB’s ancestors did its tangling, then called synthesizing, differently. Pierre-Arnoul de Marneffe’s language of “holons” envisioned a system for writing structured programs that included sophisticated compilation techniques and a notion of scope for the code chunks. We glance at a few of these long-dormant ideas from WEB’s predecessor and note their reappearance in the literate programming tool Inweb.
