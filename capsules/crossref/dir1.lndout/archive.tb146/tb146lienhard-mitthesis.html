Title: Modernizing MIT's thesis template: mitthesis.cls

Summary: thesis template construction, including extensive tagging, rendering in HTML/CSS, and expl3 coding.

Full text of article: available to TUG members (join TUG); will be publicly available after the next issue is published.

Author: John H. Lienhard

Publication: TUGboat volume 47, number 2 (2026), pages 212–221

DOI (this page): 10.47397/tb/47-2/tb146lienhard-mitthesis
(previous doi - next doi)

Category: Accessibility

Difficulty: Intermediate Plus

Abstract: A few years ago, I began an update to MIT’s LaTeX template for theses and dissertations. The previous template had been written in LaTeX 2.09 in the 1980s and patched for LaTeX2e by various students during the 1990s. My work quickly turned into a complete rewrite. My goals were to use current LaTeX methods and to produce tagged PDF while still supporting users who have minimal knowledge of LaTeX and typesetting. In this note, I discuss some of the considerations that have shaped the current template, the implementation of tagging for non-standard document elements, rendering in HTML/CSS, and some aspects of expl3 coding.
